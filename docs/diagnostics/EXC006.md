# EXC006: Try/catch is unnecessary for a `[DoesNotThrow]` call

A `try` statement has at least one `catch` clause, and every reachable
operation in its `try` body is either non-throwing or a call, constructor,
or accessor whose effective contract is `[DoesNotThrow]`. At least one of
those `[DoesNotThrow]` operations is reachable, so the catch clauses cannot
observe a tracked exception.

Remove the `catch` clauses. When the `try` has no `finally`, remove the `try`
as well. A `finally` block can stay; it does not make the catch clauses
necessary.

The warning is limited to try bodies the analyzer can fully account for.
These operations suppress EXC006:

- an explicit `throw` or `throw;`
- a `[Throws]` call, operator, or conversion
- a call, constructor, accessor, or user-defined operator with no
  Exceptionable contract
- an open virtual, abstract, or unsealed override call (`base` access is
  statically bound)
- a nested `try`
- an operation outside the non-throwing set, such as field access, element
  access, arithmetic, loops, `await`, `foreach`, `lock`, or `using`

Non-throwing operations include literals, locals, parameters, `this`, the
receiver of `?.`, discards, `nameof`, `typeof`, local assignments, implicit
or `as` conversions, `if` and `?:`, `?.`, `??`, `&&`, `||`, and built-in
Boolean `!`. `nameof` does not invoke the named member. Lambdas and local
functions are not invoked by the try body, so exceptions inside them do not
keep the catch. Async and iterator methods are not analyzed. A catch around
an unreachable `[DoesNotThrow]` call is not reported.
